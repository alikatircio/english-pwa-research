# Batch F verification report

Independent verifier, 2026-10-23. Input: `batch-f.json`, `modelBlanks-f.json` and `dailyContext-f.json` (not modified). Output: `batch-f.verified.json` (27 items, all kept), `modelBlanks-f.verified.json` (27 entries) and `dailyContext-f.verified.json` (27 entries). The author's notes file was not read.

**Result: 27 verified, 0 unsure, 0 rejected.** 25 items were changed and 2 have no change. 5 commonMistakes were changed:
- 2 were removed because the form is correct English (arrangement, so-far).
- 1 was a `null` that the schema rejects (timetable).
- 2 were replaced because the old form can be correct (its-weeks-since, no-longer-works).

## Method
- **Dictionaries.** Cambridge Grammar pages were fetched with curl and grepped. They cover present perfect simple and its typical errors, past simple, past simple or present perfect, ago, since, for, just, yet, already, the future pages (present continuous, present simple, be going to), conjunctions of time, when, conditionals with if, wh-questions, reported speech (indirect), happen, a/an and the, most, there is/there are, passive forms, double negatives, adverb position, no longer, no more and any more/anymore. Oxford Learner's pages: yet (with the already/just/yet usage note), most, more, long, happen and probably. Longman pages: ago, since, most, postpone, tell, there, happen and probably.
- **SkELL.** The concordance API was used for natural example lines. SkELL splits contractions, so queries were written as `probably wo n't`.
- **Google Books Ngram.** Corpus en-2019, mean over 2010–2019, exact form. It gave the mistake ratios and the variant frequencies. Contractions were avoided in queries. The batch C benchmark (*discuss about* = 0.93%) was used.
- **Originality.** Every accepted answer, model sentence and dailyContext sentence was checked against text extracted from both books. The check looked for shared 5-word sequences, and every hit was read in context. Four sentences were close to book example or exercise lines and were rewritten or removed (see below). The rest of the hits were generic frames ("could you tell me how", "at the end of the"). No book text is quoted in this report or in any verifiedNote.
- **Build.** `npm run content:build` was run over all `batch-*.verified.json` with all overlays. Afterwards `git checkout content/shipped-ids.lock.json` was run.

## Rule checks applied to every item
- **Rule 6b.** AmE past simple is accepted only with just and yet (present-perfect-just-sent, hasnt-replied-yet). Oxford *yet* supports both: "(North American English) I didn't receive a letter from him yet", and its usage note says already/just/yet also go with the simple past in NAmE. With so far, since, for and ago, no past-simple variant is accepted. No commonMistake is an AmE form.
- **Normaliser.** It expands I've, it's (= it is), there's, won't and the n't forms. It does not expand *there'll*, *deployment's* or *Deniz's*, so those variants are listed explicitly.
- **commonMistake.** It is never in acceptedAnswers (C4). Each one is backed by a dictionary "Not:" or "Don't say" line, a low Ngram ratio, or non-native web lines.
- **noteTr and checklistTr.** Every noteTr has at most 2 sentences and no "..." ellipsis. Every checklistTr has at most 3 entries, and the first entry is the point that the cloze blanks.
